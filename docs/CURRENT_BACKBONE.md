The core contribution is the methodology for constructing a smart semantic architectural possibility space and intelligent interaction between that space and an existing advanced LLM. The space should let architectural strategies exist robustly yet abstractly, with meanings and relationships useful before expensive realization. ## Current workflow

1. **Smart semantic sample space.** Support meaningful initial strategic reasoning before detailed dimensional realization.
2. **Exploration with iterative briefs.** The LLM obtains or composes strategies, navigates and searches the space, and responds to repeated user input.
3. **Strategy shortlist.** Choose directions worth developing. The required level of strategic certainty and useful cheap checks remain to be established.
4. **Realization.** The LLM develops selected strategies into real dimensions and arrangements, using relevant computational tools and checks.
5. **Further user input.** Selections and/or updated briefs guide subsequent work.
6. **Revise/archive/focus.** Develop existing listed cases while preserving useful alternatives and evidence.
7. **Re-search.** Reopen the broader sample space to seek a better strategic landing; work is not confined to variants of the current favorite.
8. **Evolve and test again.** Iterate development, realization, feedback and search toward a contextual local optimum.

Feedback can occur throughout. The endpoint depends on current intent, constraints, explored alternatives and budget; a stopping rule and evidence of local optimality remain research questions. Cheap targeted probes can inform shortlisting without fully realizing every possibility.

## Strategic certainty and realization budget

The user identified this as a major priority: establish and test a good level of certainty in strategic cases before expensive realization.

Realization effort could depend on initial confidence and how promising or preferred a strategy appears. No formula, confidence threshold or monotonic allocation policy is chosen. Distinguish feasibility evidence, expected benefit, user interest, and unresolved uncertainty. Model confidence alone is not a calibrated probability or feasibility certificate.

Questions: which cheap checks predict realization success; how often they fail; what evidence warrants deeper work; and how realized contradictions affect related hypotheses. A promising uncertain strategy might justify a small informative probe; this is an experiment candidate, not an agreed rule.

## Governing working principles

1. Prioritize intelligence and meaningful reasoning. More automation/branding is insufficient; simple Markdown plus an advanced LLM is a meaningful baseline.
2. The user owns the conceptual spine. Explain mathematics, logic and mechanisms before implementation; code can test ideas. Learn missing foundations first to avoid opaque systems and costly reverse engineering.
3. Investigate traceable, advanceable strategic reasoning in a pseudo mind space.
4. Preserve repeated briefing, selection and iteration, with preferences/constraints focusing contextual local search.
5. Test whether existing LLM semantic/strategic knowledge can support accurate, inexpensive proposals with limited project-specific examples. Reliable dimensional realization is also a hypothesis.
6. Research cognition/neuroscience and ML independently of the user's illustrative examples. Separate evidence, analogy and proposed application.
7. Begin with baselines and develop incrementally under real constraints.
8. Research both the construction methodology of the semantic space and how an LLM interacts with it.
9. Teach each concept through its brief meaning, why it matters and what it could contribute or solve, so the user directs synthesis.

A finite/exhaustive space requires a declared bounded domain, not a claim to cover all architecture.
