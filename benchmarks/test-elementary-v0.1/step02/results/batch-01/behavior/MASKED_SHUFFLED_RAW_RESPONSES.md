- "Width 80 ft": read as the depth of the bar. Since the brief wins over the 68-ft default (30 + 8 +

8 + 36 section would also meet the brief, but the rooms would be deeper than daylight reaches

- "Mass ratio": read as short side divided by long side.
- Commons height: "double height" is 2 × 14 = 28 ft. With about 5-ft long-span trusses, the gym
has roughly 22–23 ft clear, which is typical for an elementary school.
- Commons north band: treated as one tall storey. Kitchen and custodial have 14-ft ceilings with
unfloored plenum or rooftop equipment above, which is not counted in GFA.
- Site: orientation, access, and site fit are all assumed.
Partially met or unresolved
- Media on the "top floor": media is on the top floor of its mass and directly above
administration, but on level 2, not the building's level 3. A three-storey hub would leave level 2
with no legitimate program. Filling it would mean moving art & music upstairs, against the
ground-floor preference, or moving special education out of the 80-ft bar. I think a two-storey
hub that reads as a lower "front porch" against the three-storey bar is the better trade.
- 90-ft daylight preference: the Commons is 120 ft wide. The gym's 100-ft clear dimension makes
anything under 90 ft impossible. The instructional rooms in it (art and music) all sit on the
exterior north wall.
- Commons tolerance: the Commons is close to both its length limit and its ratio limit, so it cannot
absorb any more program. Growth must go to the bar, which has about 28 ft of length left.
- Gross basis: if 66,405 SF governs, the bar would need to shrink to about 140 ft. That fails the
floor-1 room frontage unless the corridor drops back to 8 ft, which breaks the 80-ft width rule.

- Network room: it is separated from the rest of custodial, but on the same floor, so it is a
planning choice rather than a rule violation.
- Stairs, egress, and elevator counts: need a code check.

The main strengths are clear zoning and short daily trips. Every classroom has single-sided north or south
