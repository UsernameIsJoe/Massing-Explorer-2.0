7. Architectural merits
The main strength is that the three-mass requirement becomes an operational strategy rather than
arbitrary sculpting. The school separates learning, civic/creative, and assembly/service uses while
joining them at one controlled hinge.
The 80-ft learning bars satisfy the dimensional requirement without fighting the preferred daylight
width. Kindergarten and most special education are kept at grade. Administration genuinely controls the
building rather than being merely somewhere on the ground floor. Art/music gain both ground access
and proximity to stage/assembly uses. The gym, dining, kitchen and maintenance functions form a
coherent high-volume/service zone instead of fragmenting noisy or heavily serviced spaces through the
classroom building.
The stepped upper floors of Mass B are useful volumetrically: the building is three stories where the
program benefits from vertical stacking, but unnecessary upper-floor area is not repeated across the
entire ground-floor footprint.
8. Trade-offs and unresolved feasibility questions

should be confirmed before design freeze.

separately stated 1.15 area adjustment × 1.50 grossing parameters, yielding approximately 76,366 SF,
while preserving the workbook's separate 66,405-SF displayed gross value as a source datum rather than
silently reconciling it.
No site boundary, orientation, topography or access geometry is provided. The three-mass arrangement
should therefore be treated as rotatable: once a site is known, the long classroom façades should be
oriented for useful daylight, the service side of Mass C positioned toward deliveries, and
kindergarten/special-education ground-floor rooms connected to appropriate secure outdoor areas.

thicknesses, plumbing/toilet counts beyond programmed rooms, accessibility, fire separation, egress

7. Merits and trade-offs
Merits:
- Clear zoning. Quiet learning is stacked in one disciplined bar. Noisy, large-span and
community-use spaces are in another.
- A single controlled entry. It serves both the school day and after-hours use.
- A strong civic heart. Lobby, cafeteria and stage read as one connected commons.
- Simple structure. A uniform classroom grid in A and long spans confined to B.

- Areas are verified. Every floor of A has been run-checked against actual room widths, so
the plan is not just an area tally.
Trade-offs:
- B is wider than the 90-ft daylight preference. I accepted this because the only rooms in
its deep zone are the gym and cafeteria, both double height and lit by clerestories, and
the alternative creates worse problems. That alternative is a 90 × 196 ft B, which fits only
if part of custodial moves to a mezzanine or into C. It also pushes B to 59.7 m and splits
the service functions.
- Library access. Putting media on floor 3, as the brief asks, makes it less convenient for
community evening use than a ground-floor library would be.
- Grade 3 is split across floors 2 and 3. This happens because special education takes
most of floor 2.
- Music is about 60 ft from the stage, reached via the west circulation strip. I chose this
to keep music away from the admin wall and to give the court a north-lit arts edge.
- Admin shares a wall with B's link lobby and stage storage. It needs acoustic separation.
